Design an orbit. Fire the engine. Watch the Earth turn underneath.

Pick a real kind of orbit or shape your own; the explorer explains in plain words what you are seeing. The numbers come from the same mathematics we test against our released packages.

Model: two bodies plus the average effect of the Earth's bulge (J2). Not included: air drag, the pull of the Sun and the Moon, the real date (the Greenwich meridian starts under the first point). The mathematics is tested against the released S.T.A.R. packages star_elements, star_kepler and star_j2 (148 checks; largest difference 0.26 micrometres).

Questions to ask any tool — and S.T.A.R.'s answers

Before you trust a number, ask where it came from. These are the answers S.T.A.R. can show, with a link to the proof.

Where does my data go?
Nowhere: the Workspace runs on your computer, and its code is open. See the proof →
Which method and version produced this number?
The receipt names the package and its version, and lists every change made to your input. See the proof →
Was it compared with independent programs?
Every released package was compared with at least one independent library; which ones, and how closely they agree, is published. See the proof →
What happens with bad input?
The Workspace tools refuse it clearly instead of returning a wrong number — tested: for example "1 2 3 4" is refused as a coordinate, not read as 1234. See the proof →
What are its limits?
Written on every tool and package page, in plain words. See the proof →
Can I keep using it if S.T.A.R. stops?
Yes: the packages' source code and installable files are yours to keep (Apache-2.0). See the proof →

We publish what went wrong

Every defect found in our released software is listed — the open ones too, and for the fixed ones the version that fixes them, with a test that keeps them fixed.

  • OPEN 13 older packages: some wrong inputs (for example an empty value) stop with a generic error instead of the documented clear one. No wrong result is returned.
  • FIXED After four products were released, our software catalogue still said 0 released and 4 in development, and the home page 6 tools instead of 10. The numbers now come from the products register, and the build stops if any page says otherwise.
  • FIXED Our Research page described a tool check on a 4-bit counter of 14 cells; the stored run is a 4-bit adder of 20 cells. Corrected, and the numbers are now read from the stored logs at every build.
