Interactive · 3D · computed in your browser

Orbit design and manoeuvres in 3D: configure an orbit, apply a burn, observe the ground track.

Select a reference orbit or define your own parameters; the explorer reports period, ground-track shift and nodal regression in plain terms. Its mathematics is tested against our released packages and against two independent programs.

Model: two-body motion plus the secular effect of the Earth's oblateness (J2). Not included: atmospheric drag, solar and lunar gravity, the actual date (the Greenwich meridian starts under the first point). The mathematics is tested against the released S.T.A.R. packages star_elements, star_kepler, star_j2 and star_twobody, and against two independent programs: a numerical integration of the J2 equations of motion and SGP4 (198 checks; largest propagation difference 0.26 micrometres). The satellite is a symbol drawn far larger than real: its body follows the local orbital frame, its solar arrays turn towards the illustrative Sun, and its sensor cone (20 degrees, narrowed to the Earth's disk from high orbits) is illustrative (2581 checks on the 3D models).

Questions to ask of any calculation tool, and S.T.A.R.'s answers

Before relying on a number, establish where it came from. These are the answers S.T.A.R. can document, each linked to its evidence.

Where does my data go?
It stays on your device: the Workspace runs locally and its code is open. See the proof →
Which method and version produced this number?
The receipt names the package and its version, and lists every change made to your input. See the proof →
Was it compared with independent programs?
Every released package was compared with at least one independent library; which ones, and how closely they agree, is published. See the proof →
What happens with invalid input?
The Workspace tools reject it explicitly instead of returning a wrong number. Tested: for example, "1 2 3 4" is rejected as a coordinate, not read as 1234. See the proof →
What are its limits?
Stated on every tool and package page, in plain language. See the proof →
Can I keep using it if S.T.A.R. stops?
Yes: the packages' source code and installable files remain yours (Apache-2.0). See the proof →

Recorded defects, published

Every defect found in our released software is listed, open ones included; each fixed defect names the version that fixes it and the test that keeps it fixed.

  • OPEN 13 older packages: some wrong inputs (for example an empty value) stop with a generic error instead of the documented clear one. No wrong result is returned.
  • FIXED Our own PDF checker raised false alarms on correct pages; it was corrected and proved again on a deliberately broken file.
  • FIXED The 'optimum' of the Moon mission missed the Moon by 10,326 km because of a rounding of one tenth of a metre per second; a test now plays the button's own numbers.
