What the states mean

Every product and module on this site carries one of seven states; Preview marks a Workspace tool not yet released. Research results carry the labels of their method instead (Hypothesis, Computed, Simulated, Measured). Where a registry record exists, the state is computed from it; the state says what has been checked, not how promising the idea is.

StateWhat it meansWhat it takesWhat it does not mean
Released & VerifiedPublished, versioned and checked against references.Every gate of the registry passed, including a mutation score of at least 0.85 and at least two independent implementations that agree within a stated tolerance.A certification, a qualification for aircraft or spacecraft, or the absence of defects: defects found after release are listed in the failure observatory.
ReleasedPublished and supported.Its own tests and acceptance checks pass; the two-independent-implementations gate is not required.Checked against an independent reference.
BetaUsable, verified against references, still changing.Working code with tests and at least one comparison with a reference; some release gates still open.Stable interfaces or results that will not move.
PrototypeWorking code that shows the idea.It runs and has tests for what it claims.Verified against a reference, or ready for use.
ResearchAn open question with a written method.A plan, a preregistered criterion where there is a measurement, and the data it will use.A result: no number is published before the measurement.
PlannedSpecified, not built.A written specification.A date or a promise.
BlockedStopped by a named obstacle.The obstacle and what would remove it, stated on the item's page.Abandoned.

Words kept apart

A state says how far an item has been checked. Labels on a single result say where that number came from: SIMULATED, PREDICTED, CROSSCHECKED, FORMALLY VERIFIED, EXPERIMENTALLY VALIDATED. An item can be Beta and still publish a CROSSCHECKED number; it cannot be called Released & Verified until every gate is passed.
