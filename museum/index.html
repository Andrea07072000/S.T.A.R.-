Interactive museum

Exhibits to take in hand: open a processor layer by layer down to a single transistor, launch spacecraft, build circuits, change the data and see the model respond. Each exhibit states the model behind it and its limits.

Chips

Inside a processor

Ten layers from heatsink to motherboard, then the die's wiring and one transistor, at three magnifications.

Representative model

Limits: Representative geometry and typical ranges from public packaging and process literature, not a specific product. Bumps and BGA balls are shown on coarser grids; vias are drawn at a fraction of the real crossings. The transistor current follows a declared idealised model (threshold 0.3 V, subthreshold slope 70 mV per decade).

Build a circuit

Logic gates on a board: wire NAND gates into XOR, then into the adder at the heart of every processor.

Simulation of a declared model

Limits: Ideal combinational logic: no propagation delay, no fan-out limits, unconnected inputs read 0. The check evaluates every combination of the inputs. Tested: NOT, XOR (4 NAND) and a full adder (9 NAND) built from NAND gates match their truth tables for every input; a missing wire and a loop are detected.

Two ways to package a chip

A wire-bonded package with gold wires, and an AI accelerator with memory stacks you can lift apart.

Representative model

Limits: Representative proportions from public packaging literature, scene unit 1 mm; no specific product. Wires, bumps and vias are fewer and thicker than true so that they remain visible.

A bit that leaks

Write bits into a DRAM array, warm it, stop the refresh, and see the memory forget.

Representative model

Limits: 128 cells with retention times drawn from a log-normal spread (median 64 s at 45 °C), halving for every 10 °C; a cell reads 1 above half charge. Representative of published DRAM behaviour, not of a specific product; real arrays hold billions of cells and use error-correcting codes. Time can run up to 100 times faster than real. Tested: the halving per 10 °C, the median, refresh shortly before and shortly after the threshold, every one of 20,000 cells holding 64 ms at 85 °C, and half lost after 44 s without refresh at 45 °C.

From the wafer to the atom

One continuous descent from a 300 mm wafer to the silicon atoms, everything at its true size.

Representative model

Limits: Representative dimensions of a recent logic process from public literature, not of a specific product; process names (such as “3 nm”) no longer equal any physical length. Every level and reference object is at its true size relative to the others; colours are illustrative and the rotation is part of the presentation. Tested: the levels' order, the lengths, and the geometry of the silicon lattice.

Printing with light

Draw a mask and see what 193 nm light, EUV and high-NA EUV can print at a few tens of nanometres.

Representative model

Limits: Scalar, coherent, on-axis imaging of a binary mask through an ideal circular pupil, a fixed resist threshold, no mask 3D effects, aberrations, flare or stochastic effects. Production tools use off-axis illumination and computational masks that print periods down to about half the coherent limit shown here. Tested: the limits of 143, 41 and 25 nm, a grating printed or lost depending on the source, a large pad printed faithfully, and the 2D FFT.

Where a chip gets hot

Paint power onto a silicon die and find the hot spot that would force it to slow down.

Representative model

Limits: One layer of silicon 0.5 mm thick, 20 × 20 mm, conductivity 150 W/m·K, a uniform conductance to the ambient (35 °C) through the package and cooling; no package layers, no hot-spot heat spreaders, no transients (the field shown settles by iteration, not in real time). Power maps are illustrative. Tested: uniform heating against the closed form, the energy balance, and the spreading length √(k t / G) against the decay of a hot spot.

Inside a processor's assembly line

Five stages, one instruction per cycle: reorder a program until no cycle is wasted.

Representative model

Limits: The textbook five-stage in-order pipeline with a register file written and read in the same cycle, one-cycle memory, no caches or structural hazards; forwarding removes all stalls except one after a load. Real cores issue several instructions per cycle, out of order, with dozens of stages. The best order is found by searching every reordering that keeps the meaning. Tested: one instruction per cycle without dependences, two stalls without forwarding, the load-use stall, the branch penalty, and a reordering that removes a stall.

Space

Orbital slingshot

Pull a spacecraft back and release it: circular orbit, transfer, escape, the Moon.

Simulation of a declared model

Limits: The Earth is a point mass held fixed; the Moon (when shown) moves on a circular orbit in the same plane and pulls the spacecraft; no atmosphere, no Sun, no engine burn duration. Speeds are given at 400 km altitude. The numbers come from the vis-viva relation and a fourth-order Runge-Kutta integration tested against Kepler's period and energy conservation.

Gravity sandbox

Create bodies, throw them, see orbits form and break: the figure-eight, binaries, planets.

Simulation of a declared model

Limits: Newton's gravity in a plane, with G = 1 and a small softening length (0.01) that keeps close passes finite; fourth-order Runge-Kutta. The energy drift shows how well the integrator keeps the energy: it changes only when bodies are added, moved, thrown or merged. Tested: the figure-eight returns to its start after one period within 1e-4, energy and momentum kept to 1e-9.

Build a launcher

Stack stages, size their tanks, choose engines, and see whether the payload reaches orbit.

Simulation of a declared model

Limits: Delta-v from Tsiolkovsky's equation, stage by stage, with vacuum specific impulses (kerosene-oxygen 311 s, hydrogen-oxygen 450 s, solid 270 s) and structural ratios (dry mass over stage mass) of 7 %, 10 % and 12 %. The budgets include typical losses to gravity and atmospheric drag; thrust, throttling and the real ascent path are not modelled. The launch is an animation of the staging sequence. Tested: closed form, two stages beat one of the same propellant, and the best split gives equal mass ratios.

The five balance points

Launch probes across the potential of two bodies in orbit and find where they can stay.

Simulation of a declared model

Limits: Circular restricted three-body problem in the rotating frame, units where the distance and the angular velocity of the pair are 1; integrated by fourth-order Runge-Kutta with steps of at most 0.002, smaller near the bodies. The surface is cut at the wells so that it stays readable; bodies are drawn much larger than their true size. Tested: no net force at the five points, the published positions of L1–L3 for Earth–Moon and Sun–Jupiter, Jacobi constant kept, L4 stable below Routh's ratio and unstable above it, L1 unstable.

The gravitational slingshot

Aim a probe past Jupiter and borrow its orbital speed, with no propellant.

Simulation of a declared model

Limits: Patched conics: during the flyby only Jupiter's gravity acts, and Jupiter moves in a straight line at 13.07 km/s; the Sun's pull over these few days is neglected. Jupiter is drawn four times larger than true so that it remains visible; the probe is a point. Integrated by fourth-order Runge-Kutta with steps proportional to the distance. Tested: energy kept in Jupiter's frame, closest approach and turn angle equal to the hyperbola's formulas, the speed change within 2 v∞ sin(δ/2), and impacts detected.

Design a constellation

Choose planes, satellites and altitude and see, live, which parts of the Earth they can see.

Simulation of a declared model

Limits: Walker-delta patterns of circular orbits around a spherical Earth; a place sees a satellite when it is above the minimum elevation. Coverage is instantaneous, on a grid of 72 × 144 cells weighted by area, without the Earth's rotation, perturbations, eclipses or links between satellites. Patterns are like the named systems, not their exact designs. Tested: footprint angles, orbital periods, the geometry of the pattern, one satellite's coverage against the spherical cap, and four satellites in view over 97 % of the Earth for the GPS-like pattern.

A lens made of gravity

Move a dark mass across the sky and bend the light of a galaxy into arcs and an Einstein ring.

Simulation of a declared model

Limits: A single point mass in the thin-lens, weak-field approximation; angles in units of the Einstein radius, whose true size depends on the mass and the distances (about one thousandth of an arcsecond for a star halfway to the Galactic centre). The dark disc marks the lens and is not to scale; the star field and the galaxy are drawn procedurally. Tested: the 1.75″ bending of light at the Sun's edge measured in 1919, both images mapping back to the source, the magnification formula against the area ratio of the mapping, and the Einstein radius of a star lens.

When to leave for Mars

11,550 trajectories in one chart: pick a launch date and a flight time and fly to Mars.

Simulation of a declared model

Limits: Circular, coplanar orbits for the Earth and Mars placed by their mean longitudes, the Sun's gravity only, single-revolution prograde transfers; Mars's eccentricity and inclination change the real windows' dates and energies by several days and some km²/s². The burn from low orbit assumes a 200 km parking orbit. Tested: Lambert's solution recovers the velocity of a propagated orbit, the Hohmann transfer of 259 days, the 780-day synodic period, and the November 2026 window.

Coming home

Bring a capsule back from orbit, the Moon or Mars through a corridor two degrees wide.

Simulation of a declared model

Limits: Planar flight over a spherical Earth with an exponential atmosphere (scale height 7.2 km), constant aerodynamic coefficients (atmospheric drag and lift), the lift rolled down while climbing as the only guidance, heating at the stagnation point by the Sutton–Graves relation without radiation. Altitudes are drawn eight times higher than true. Vehicles are representative, not the real capsules' data. Tested: Allen–Eggers peak deceleration for steep entries, its independence from the vehicle, the lunar-return corridor of about −5.4° to −7.2° (Apollo's was about −5.2° to −7.2°), skip-out when too shallow, and a gentle return from orbit.

The shape of a nozzle

Pull a rocket bell wider or narrower and fly it from the launch pad to vacuum.

Simulation of a declared model

Limits: Ideal one-dimensional isentropic flow of a perfect gas with frozen composition, chamber at 100 bar, representative chamber temperatures and molar masses; no losses from friction, divergence, incomplete combustion or cooling. Separation by Summerfield's criterion (exit pressure below 40 % of ambient). Atmosphere exponential (scale height 7.4 km). The plume shape is illustrative. Tested: the area–Mach relation, c* of kerosene and hydrogen, vacuum and sea-level specific impulse, separation of a vacuum nozzle at sea level, and the optimum bell exhausting at ambient pressure.

The colour of stars

Build a star by its mass, or place one on the Hertzsprung–Russell diagram, and see its spectrum, size and lifetime.

Simulation of a declared model

Limits: Black-body spectra and colours (real spectra have absorption lines); main-sequence luminosity, radius and lifetime from simple power-law fits of mass, good to a factor of a few at the extremes; known stars from published temperatures and luminosities, rounded. The star is drawn at the cube root of its radius so that every size fits. Tested: Wien's peak of the Sun, Planck's maximum against Wien, the integral of Planck against σT⁴, the Sun's luminosity, and main-sequence lifetimes.

Quantum

One qubit in the hand

Place a quantum state anywhere on the Bloch sphere, turn it with gates, measure it.

Simulation of a declared model

Limits: One ideal qubit: no noise, no decoherence, exact amplitudes. The global phase is not shown, as on the sphere. Shots are drawn by a seeded generator, so a repeated run gives the same counts. The rotation drawn for each gate equals the gate's matrix on the amplitudes to 1e-12 for every gate and 400 test states; the matrices are checked against Qiskit.

Two slits, one photon at a time

Photons land one by one; the interference pattern appears only in their distribution. Then find out which slit they took.

Simulation of a declared model

Limits: Far-field (Fraunhofer) intensity of two slits; photon arrivals are drawn from it at random, as quantum mechanics predicts their distribution and not each position. The wave drawn on the table uses a wavelength enlarged several thousand times so that it can be seen, and two point sources; the screen uses the real wavelength and geometry. The bright fringes sit slightly inside λL/d where the single-slit envelope falls. Tested: the formula equals a direct Huygens-Fresnel sum within 1 % at 1 m; the arrivals follow the intensity (chi-square per bin near 1 over 200,000 photons).

Through the wall

A quantum wave packet meets a barrier higher than its energy, and part of it comes out the other side.

Simulation of a declared model

Limits: One particle in one dimension, units with ħ = m = 1, a rectangular barrier; split-step Fourier on 2,048 points with absorbing edges. The expected value averages the plane-wave transmission coefficient over the packet's momenta. Tested on a finer grid: the transmitted share matches the expectation within 1 % for four barriers, and the norm is kept to 1e-9.

The quantum composer

Set gates on three qubits and see eight amplitudes rise as columns of light; meet six challenges.

Simulation of a declared model

Limits: An exact simulation of three ideal qubits: no noise, no decoherence; the simulator is the one of the circuit lab, checked against Qiskit. A measurement draws 1000 outcomes from the computed probabilities. Qubit q0 is the rightmost digit of each label (|q2 q1 q0⟩). Tested: placement rules, each challenge reached with its stated number of gates and not reachable with one fewer, the link format and its refusals.

Protecting a qubit

Spread one bit over many qubits, let noise flip them, and correct it from parity checks alone.

Simulation of a declared model

Limits: The repetition code against bit flips only, with independent errors and perfect parity checks; real quantum codes (such as the surface code) must also handle phase flips and faulty measurements, which lowers their threshold to about 1 % instead of 50 %. Tested: the closed formula, decoding from the syndrome alone against majority voting on all 128 patterns of 7 qubits, and 200,000 simulated rounds.

Physics and sound

Draw a sound

Trace a wave and hear it; raise or lower its harmonics and see the shape follow.

Simulation of a declared model

Limits: One period sampled at 256 points; the Fourier coefficients come from an FFT tested against the direct discrete transform. Only the first 16 harmonics are shown and played, so a drawn corner is rounded in the blue line. Tested: square-wave harmonics 4/(nπ) with the even ones zero, sawtooth harmonics 2/(nπ), and exact resynthesis of a sampled signal.

Chaos in the hand

Lift a double pendulum and let it go next to a twin that starts one millionth of a radian away.

Simulation of a declared model

Limits: Point masses of 1 kg on massless rods of 1 m, g = 9.81 m/s², no friction; Lagrange's equations integrated by fourth-order Runge-Kutta in steps of 0.5 ms. Tested: the energy is kept within 1e-7 over 10 s of chaotic motion, and the slow normal mode has the period of the closed form.

Data and studio

Your data in three dimensions

Paste a table and see it take shape in 3D; switch an axis to logarithmic and a law appears.

Representative model

Limits: Up to 400 rows; the line is ordinary least squares on the axes as shown (logarithms when an axis is logarithmic), so on two logarithmic axes it is a power law and on a logarithmic vertical axis an exponential. Sample values are widely published figures, rounded: planetary data from NASA's fact sheet, transistor counts as announced by each manufacturer, payloads as published by each operator. Tested: numbers in English and Italian notation, the separators, quoted fields, Kepler's exponent 1.5 and a Moore doubling time near two years.

The animation studio

Place spacecraft, planets and chips on a timeline and let the studio compute the motion; share it with a link.

Representative model

Limits: Up to 8 objects, 24 keys each, 30 seconds. Smooth motion uses Catmull-Rom splines through every key (position) and eased interpolation (turn and size); straight motion interpolates linearly. Objects are illustrative models, not to scale. A link stores the keys rounded to 0.01 in the address; on opening, it is read with fixed limits and anything malformed is refused. Tested: paths through every key, the linear midpoint, encoding and decoding, eleven malformed or oversized links refused, out-of-range values clamped.

Challenge hall

Twelve rounds of space and chips

Order the cards, estimate on a logarithmic scale, choose places on the map: every answer scored and explained.

Representative model

Limits: Answers are widely published values, rounded. An estimate scores 100 when exact and 0 when off by a factor of 30 or more, linearly in the logarithm; an ordering scores the share of pairs in the right order; a place scores by its latitude. Your best result is kept only in this browser. Tested: the scoring, every round's values, and two answers recomputed from physics (orbital speed, light time).

The next frontier

The digital twin

A satellite and its twin on the ground: inject a fault and the twin finds it from the telemetry alone.

Simulation of a declared model

Limits: One thermal node: heat capacity 9 kJ/K, sunlight 110 W, electronics 45 W, heater 80 W on a 285–289 K thermostat, radiation to space as a linear conductance; orbit 92 minutes with 38 % in eclipse; sensor noise 0.5 K. The twin is a two-state Kalman filter (temperature and unexplained power) and plans with the worst unexplained power seen over the last orbit. A real twin has hundreds of nodes and many sensors. Tested: the healthy cycle stays above 280 K with the learned power near zero, each fault is detected, and for a failed heater the twin warns before the battery reaches 0 °C.

The machine that rediscovers laws

Measurements in, a law out, then verified on data the machine never saw: Kepler, Newton, Stefan–Boltzmann.

Simulation of a declared model

Limits: The search space is power laws of up to three variables with exponents from −4 to 4 in steps of ½: the machine cannot find a law outside it, and says so when verification fails. Each candidate is fitted by least squares in logarithms and scored by its error plus a small penalty for complexity. Datasets are generated from the known laws with the chosen noise. Real automated discovery explores far larger spaces; the method shown, propose, fit, prefer simplicity, verify on unseen data, is the same. Tested: five laws rediscovered with the right exponents and verified on 24 unseen points, the constants 2π/√g and G recovered, and the Pareto front.

A window at nearly the speed of light

Accelerate at 1 g towards the stars and see the sky gather ahead while the ship's clock falls behind Earth's.

Concept, not a product

Limits: Special relativity, flat space-time, a ship of constant proper acceleration with a turnaround at the midpoint; the energy needed is not modelled (no known propulsion can provide it). The star field is generated with realistic colours and brightnesses but not from a catalogue; aberration and the Doppler shift are applied to every star, brightness with a beaming factor D³ (with a floor so that the dimmed stars behind stay visible). Tested: Proxima Centauri in 3.5 years on board and 5.9 on Earth at 1 g, the Galactic centre in about 20 years on board, the speed after a year, γ² (1 − β²) = 1, aberration and Doppler factors.

A lift to orbit

A cable to 144,000 km at true scale: pick a material, climb it, release a payload and follow its orbit.

Concept, not a product

Limits: An ideal tapered cable of constant stress anchored at the equator, with no counterweight (it ends where the pulls balance, about 144,000 km up); the Earth spherical, no atmosphere, no Moon or Sun. Material strengths are working values from datasheets; the two nanotube cases are laboratory or projected values, not available materials. The cable's width is drawn on a compressed scale; lengths are true. Time runs at 6 hours per second. Tested: the geostationary radius, the taper of each material, the breaking length of steel, the apparent gravity, the release orbits and the escape radius of 53,000 km.

A star in a bottle

Temperature, density, confinement: bring a fusion plasma past breakeven on Lawson's diagram.

Simulation of a declared model

Limits: A zero-dimensional power balance of a 50/50 deuterium-tritium plasma: uniform temperature and density, losses only through the confinement time, radiation, impurities and profiles neglected; Q is the plasma's fusion power over the external heating power, not the plant's electricity balance. Reactivity from the Bosch–Hale fit. The machine drawn is illustrative. Tested: the reactivity at 10 keV and its peak, the ignition minimum of 2.8 × 10²¹ keV·s/m³ near 14 keV, and the triple products for Q = 1 and Q = 10.

Keeping low orbit usable

Launch rate, disposal and cleanup: run two centuries of orbital debris and find the policies that avoid a cascade.

Representative model

Limits: A one-shell source–sink model in the spirit of Kessler (1978) and of the policy studies that followed: derelicts and fragments decaying by atmospheric drag (300 and 100 years), catastrophic collisions only when an intact object is hit (rate c·D·(D + F)), 1,500 fragments each. Parameters are chosen to give today's order of magnitude, not fitted to any agency's model, and the result is a comparison of policies, not a forecast. Active satellites are assumed to avoid collisions. Tested: the threshold of 3,333 derelicts, a collision every five or six years today, a cascade with 95 % disposal at 3,000 launches a year, slow growth at 99 %, and a falling population when 40 derelicts a year are removed.

A quantum internet

Share entangled photons between two cities: direct fibre, a chain of quantum repeaters, or a satellite.

Representative model

Limits: Fibre loss of 0.2 dB/km, a 1 GHz source, 90 % detectors; repeaters with ideal memories, swaps that succeed 90 % of the time and one attempt per light round trip over a segment; a satellite at 500 km with two 35 dB downlinks and a 6 MHz source, usable only when both cities see it above 15° (at most about 2,500 km apart), weather ignored. No real network is modelled; the satellite figures are of the order reported for the Micius experiments (2017). Tested: the fibre loss, the PLOB bound, the waiting time for all segments, a 16-segment chain against a direct link over 1,000 km, and great-circle distances.

Solar power from orbit

A collector in permanent sunlight beams microwaves to the ground: follow every loss along the chain.

Concept, not a product

Limits: A single geostationary satellite at 45° N elevation geometry simplified to the full 36,000 km distance; beam collection by Goubau's relation for a Gaussian-tapered aperture; peak intensity for a uniform aperture, an upper bound; efficiencies of 30 % (cells), 80 % (DC to microwaves), 85 % (rectenna) and 95 % (grid) as typical published targets, not demonstrated at scale. Launch, assembly and cost are not modelled. Tested: Goubau's parameter and collected share, the chain of efficiencies, the peak intensity and sunlight at GEO 99 % of the year.
