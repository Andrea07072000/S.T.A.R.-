Scientific Workbench

Five released S.T.A.R. packages, run in your browser: check a satellite element set, solve a linear system exactly, read the standard atmosphere, decode a space packet and size a satellite's coverage. Nothing is uploaded: the package runs on your computer.

Satellite element set (TLE)

Paste the two lines of a NORAD element set. The parser checks every field and both checksums, and refuses what it cannot read rather than guessing.

Package: star_tle · Limits: Parses and checks the format; it does not propagate the orbit. Period and altitudes come from the mean motion (two-body).

Linear system, solved exactly

One row of the matrix per line, numbers separated by spaces; the right-hand side on the last field. Elimination is done in exact rational arithmetic and rounded once, so even ill-conditioned matrices, such as the Hilbert matrix of the example, give the correctly rounded answer.

Package: star_linsolve · Limits: Up to order 20; no least squares, no eigenvalues, no condition number.

U.S. Standard Atmosphere 1976

Temperature, pressure, density and speed of sound at a geometric altitude, from sea level to 86 km.

Package: star_atmosphere · Limits: Below 86 km only; the standard atmosphere is a mean model, not the weather of a given day.

CCSDS space packet

Paste a packet in hexadecimal. The primary header is decoded field by field (CCSDS 133.0-B-2) and the length is checked against the octets received.

Package: star_spacepacket · Limits: Primary header and data field; the secondary header and the payload are shown as octets, not interpreted.

Satellite coverage

What a satellite at a given altitude sees above a minimum elevation: angles, distance, the radius of its footprint and the share of the Earth it covers.

Package: star_coverage · Limits: Spherical Earth of 6,378.137 km; no terrain, no atmosphere.
