What's new

What changed on this website, newest first.

3 November 2026 — Clearer plans, one button for local data, corrected promises

  • Plans: Free is available now; Pro and Enterprise are coming soon; tailor-made work and research partnerships start from a conversation. No paid work starts before a written agreement.
  • Earlier pages promised a reply within 2 working days and strict confidentiality in isolated environments. We could not show evidence for these, so they are withdrawn: a first answer aims to arrive within 7 days, on a best-effort basis.
  • A single control now clears everything this site stored in your browser. Receipts mask tax codes and IBANs, on screen, in the file and in print.
  • Published checksums now match every file, phones no longer scroll sideways, and the empty package of our C library is gone. Details are in the failure observatory.
  • Legal documents, version 2.2. They have not been reviewed by a lawyer.

21 October 2026 — A whole table in one pass, stronger tests of the Workspace, two language fixes

  • New in the Workspace, our fifth released product: check a whole table in one pass. Numbers, tax codes, VAT numbers, IBANs and dates of a table are checked together; you get a cleaned copy with your columns unchanged, the list of every value read differently, and one receipt. It was published first as a preview and released after its test on the published site, on a phone and on a desktop.
  • We measured how good the Workspace engine's own tests are, by mutation testing: its code was changed in 541 small ways, one at a time. The tests noticed 308 of them (57%). 99 new tests, written from what slipped through, now notice 522; each of the other 19 is explained (no input can tell it apart from the original). The new one-pass tool scores 0.99 the same way.
  • Two fixes in Italian: numbers with a decimal point were described as "decimali con la punto" (FAIL-0045), and counts did not agree with their nouns, as in "1 giorni lavorativi" (FAIL-0046). Every sentence with a count now has its singular, and a test checks each new one.

20 October 2026 — Four everyday checks, a sample report, and our commitments in writing

  • Four new free tools in the Workspace: check an Italian tax code (omocodia included), an Italian VAT number and an IBAN of any country, and count working days or set a deadline with Italian holidays, Easter and your patron saint. Each is tested against independent libraries (python-stdnum, schwifty, holidays), and every result comes with a receipt that states its limits.
  • A sample assessment report and a sample written quote show what a client receives; the report is built from our public orbit campaign XC-008.
  • Our commitments at that date (the reply time and the strict confidentiality were withdrawn on 2026-11-03, see above): a first technical reply within a few working hours and at most 2 working days; the Workspace and the packages are free; tailor-made work only on a fixed written quote per phase; strict confidentiality for client material.
  • Terms of use, version 1.4: they now describe the Workspace tools and what they cannot replace.
  • Research: a first hardware check — a CRC-32 block proved equal, for every input of its byte step, to equations derived from star_crc, and simulated on 658 messages. And a correction (FAIL-0037): the Research page described a tool check on a 4-bit counter of 14 cells; the stored run is a 4-bit adder of 20 cells. The numbers are now read from the stored logs at every build. Also corrected (FAIL-0040): after the four products were released, the software catalogue still counted 0 released products and the home page 6 tools instead of 10; these counts now come from the products register.
  • The Workspace engine now runs in a background worker: on a 60 MB file or a 49,000-row table the page keeps answering (longest pause between frames 17 ms, measured), and a Stop button ends a run. Measuring this found a defect: with very large tables the column tool showed an error instead of its result (FAIL-0042); fixed. Every package page now says whether it runs in the browser, measured by running its own tests there.
  • New first step in the Workspace: give it a table and it finds tax codes, VAT numbers, IBANs, dates, coordinates and amounts, counts the valid ones, and opens the right checks already filled in. The questionnaire now knows the document and payment checks, its third question follows your answer to the second, and it can remember your answers on this device if you tick the box (privacy and cookie notices 1.5).

20 October 2026 — Free tools, 3D labs, and corrections found by our own review

  • A new home page and menu. The Workspace: six free tools that run released S.T.A.R. packages in your browser, each result with a verification receipt, and three questions that suggest the right tool.
  • Two 3D labs: an orbit explorer (real orbits, engine burns, ground track, the story in plain words) and a lab on CPUs, GPUs, AI accelerators and a qubit. Every picture says whether it is an illustration, a simulation or a measurement.
  • Our release check counted our own Lambert solver as an independent comparison (FAIL-0036). It is corrected: star_lambert is now VERIFIED, and every package page shows the facts the release check used.
  • Every package page now states its limits; open defects are listed with the fixed ones; the research pages state the small test runs made on IBM cloud processors; the tools used for chip research were checked on a test circuit.

16 October 2026 — Clearer evidence, stricter release rules

  • The 3D view on the home page now tells its story: the satellite flies each test orbit, then the camera reaches the arrival point, where the positions computed by three implementations sit inside the 5 cm tolerance sphere. Real coastlines (Natural Earth, public domain).
  • Stricter rule for RELEASED (DEC-015): every capability must agree with at least two independent implementations besides ours, and the latest mutation score of every module must be at least 0.85, recomputed from its result file. The registry applies it itself: 81 of the 90 capabilities that were released meet it today; the other 9 are VERIFIED until they do.
  • An independent review of the whole website (13 review angles, 94 findings): links in the legal PDFs, keyboard and screen-reader use, phone layout, the C library page, the search, the forms and the legal texts were corrected. Legal documents are now version 1.3.
  • A preview image for search engines and social networks, rendered from the test data.

15 October 2026 — Italian, search, legal tools

  • All 70 package sheets in Italian; site search (Ctrl+K); structured data for search engines.
  • New legal documents (acceptable use, minimum age, payments and invoicing, consumers, data breaches, record of processing), fillable forms, a compliance sheet for every package, SBOM and citation files; 210 PDFs in a downloadable legal pack.
  • Content-Security-Policy on every page; fonts and scripts served only by this website.

15 October 2026 — The website opens

  • 70 open-source packages with a page each, downloads with fingerprints, and "Try it" running Python in the browser.
