Payments and invoicing

How paid work is agreed, documented and paid, and how to recognise a fraudulent payment request.

1. Nothing is paid on this website

The website sells nothing, has no shopping cart and never asks for card numbers, bank credentials or any payment data. The software is free.

2. How paid work is agreed

  1. You describe the question (the assessment request form helps).
  2. The owner answers with a written offer: scope, deliverables, limits, price in euro, taxes, dates.
  3. Where confidential material is involved, a non-disclosure agreement is signed first.
  4. The contract is signed by both parties. Clauses that limit liability or rights are signed a second time, as Italian law requires for standard terms (Civil Code, art. 1341 and 1342).
  5. Payment is made by bank transfer to the account written in the signed contract, on the dates it states.

3. Invoices and receipts

At this date S.T.A.R. has no VAT number. Until one is opened, occasional work is documented by a receipt for occasional self-employment, with the withholding tax and the revenue stamp that Italian law requires when they apply. Once a VAT number is opened, every invoice is issued electronically through the Italian exchange system (Sistema di Interscambio); you will be asked for your billing details (name, address, VAT or tax code, recipient code or PEC registered email) with the billing details form. Documents are kept for the period tax law requires.

4. How to recognise a fraudulent payment request

  • The owner never asks to pay through a link, by card, in crypto-assets or with gift cards.
  • Bank details are only those written in the signed contract. A request to pay a different account, even from an address that looks right, is to be treated as fraud until confirmed on a second channel you already know.
  • Report any suspicious request to Info.and.News.S.T.A.R@proton.me and, for fraud, to the Polizia Postale.

5. Late payment

Between businesses, the statutory interest for late payment applies (Legislative Decree 231/2002) unless the contract states otherwise. Nothing in this page applies to consumers more strictly than the law allows: see consumers and complaints.
