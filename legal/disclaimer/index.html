What a S.T.A.R. result establishes, and what it does not.

What a published result establishes

That, for the inputs listed and with the software versions recorded, two or more implementations gave results that agree within the tolerance stated; or, where stated, that a result matches a published reference value or a value derived by hand. Every result names its inputs, versions, tolerance and evidence file.

What it does not establish

  • That the software is correct for inputs that were not tested.
  • That two implementations are independent beyond what is declared: shared algorithms, libraries or data are stated when known.
  • Agreement between implementations is not proof that any of them is right; it is evidence that an error, if present, is shared.
  • Any certification, accreditation or qualification under ECSS, DO-178C, NASA standards or any other scheme.
  • Fitness for flight, for operational collision avoidance or for any safety-critical decision.

Maturity

S.T.A.R. software is at prototype maturity (technology readiness level 6 at most). Hardware results are obtained on emulators (Renode), not on physical boards.

Status labels

  • RELEASED: installed from a fixed version, its tests pass, compared with at least two independent libraries, mutation score of the tests at least 0.85.
  • AGREE: the implementations agree within the stated tolerance on the stated cases.
  • COMING SOON: described but not available.

Responsibility

Whoever uses a result remains responsible for the decisions taken with it. In an engineering assessment, the scope, the cases and the limits are agreed in writing before the work starts.
