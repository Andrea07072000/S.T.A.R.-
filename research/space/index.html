Space and orbits

Orbits, time, reference frames, telemetry and space-safety data — the field where S.T.A.R. started. Play with real orbits in 3D, then see how the software behind them was checked.

Interactive · 3D · computed in your browser

Design an orbit. Fire the engine. Watch the Earth turn underneath.

Pick a real kind of orbit or shape your own; the explorer explains in plain words what you are seeing. The numbers come from the same mathematics we test against our released packages.

Model: two bodies plus the average effect of the Earth's bulge (J2). Not included: air drag, the pull of the Sun and the Moon, the real date (the Greenwich meridian starts under the first point). The mathematics is tested against the released S.T.A.R. packages star_elements, star_kepler and star_j2 (148 checks; largest difference 0.26 micrometres).

One check in detail: three programs propagate the same orbit

S.T.A.R.'s orbit propagator (a fixed-step RK4 integrator, star_orbit 0.2.0) was compared with two reference integrators on four test orbits. The two references agree with each other within the 0.1 mm precision of the recorded positions; S.T.A.R. differs from them by about 1.5 cm after one orbit — inside the 5 cm tolerance, and visibly the least precise of the three. star_orbit is VERIFIED, not yet RELEASED.
