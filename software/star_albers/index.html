star-albers 0.1.0

Albers equal-area conic projection

Apache-2.0 RELEASED single file

In plain words

Albers equal-area conic projection, forward, inverse and both scale factors, for cones in either hemisphere and the tangent cone; within 1.9e-6 m of PROJ and 9.1e-7 m of PyGeodesy on 600 cases; reproduces Snyder's published coordinates and scale factors and refuses degenerate cones.

Requirements

  • R1 albers_forward and albers_inverse: Snyder's formulas (USGS PP 1395, 14-1 … 14-21), cones in either hemisphere, equal parallels for the tangent cone, the central meridian anywhere including across the date line. Reproduce Snyder's numerical example to the printed 0.1 m.
  • R2 albers_scale returns (h, k) with h·k = 1: areas are preserved; k = 1 on the standard parallels. Reproduces Snyder's h = 1.0085173 and k = 0.9915546.
  • R3 Measured on 600 cones and points on WGS-84 (60 tangent cones): forward within 1.9e-6 m of PROJ and 9.1e-7 m of PyGeodesy; inverse within 2.6e-6 m of the starting point; parallel scale within 9e-14 of PyGeodesy and 1.3e-9 of PROJ (which differentiates numerically).
  • R4 Every function returns finite floats or raises ValueError: non-numeric, boolean, NaN or infinite input, latitudes beyond ±89, longitudes beyond ±180, standard parallels on opposite sides of the equator or within 1e-3 deg of it, an ellipsoid outside the stated ranges, a point outside the image of the cone.

Limits (what is not claimed)

No false easting or northing, no named national grids. The cone constant is computed as a difference between the two standard parallels: when they are very close (0.04 deg apart in the worst case measured) about four digits are lost and the difference from the libraries reaches 2 micrometres; PyGeodesy's formulation does not have this loss. The accuracy stated is agreement with two libraries, not with a survey.
