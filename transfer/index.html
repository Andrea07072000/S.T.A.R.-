From research to everyday software

Three cases where a research question became software you can rely on, or showed where you cannot. Each one with its baseline, data, hardware, cost and limits.

Post-quantum key exchange from the standard alone

The key exchange that will protect messages, backups and connections against future quantum computers.

QuestionCan ML-KEM be written from FIPS 203 alone and match the official test vectors?
BaselineNIST ACVP vectors (the official answers) and OpenSSL 4.0.1 / pyca cryptography 48.0.1.
DataNIST ACVP-Server ML-KEM keyGen and encapDecap JSON files, downloaded 2026-10-17, fingerprints recorded.
Result240 of 240 vectors byte-exact; 500 + 500 ML-KEM-768 and 500 + 500 ML-KEM-1024 exchanges interoperable with OpenSSL in both directions.
Hardware and costIntel Core i5-7200U laptop, 2 cores; free tools; no cloud.
LimitsPure Python: slow and not hardened against timing side channels. Not for production secrets. Internal prototype, not yet a public package.

Collision probability compared with NASA's reference

The same method decides whether two satellites need to move; the habit of checking it carries to any risk number.

QuestionDoes our 2D probability-of-collision engine reproduce NASA CARA's values?
BaselineNASA CARA Analysis Tools test conjunctions (commit ce0f007) and Orekit's Laas2015 method.
Data53 public test conjunctions; 48 with probability at or above 1e-10 compared.
ResultRule fixed before the run: every case within 1e-6 relative. 11 of 48 were above, the largest 2.9e-3: the claim failed. Orekit agreed with NASA within 3.3e-8 on the same 48.
Hardware and costSame laptop; free tools.
LimitsResearch software: not an operational collision-avoidance service. The failure is published as found.

Orbit propagation: three programs compared

Satellite positions behind navigation, weather and Earth-observation services.

QuestionDo independent implementations agree on where a satellite is after one orbit with the J2 effect?
BaselineSciPy integration of the same force model and hapsira (poliastro) with its own force model.
Data4 test orbits, declared inputs, tolerance 5 cm, fixed before the run.
Result4 of 4 cases within the 5 cm tolerance. The two references agree with each other within the 0.1 mm recording precision; S.T.A.R. is about 1.5 cm from both (largest 1.49 cm), the least precise of the three.
Hardware and costSame laptop; free tools; rerun with one command from the public repository.
LimitsAgreement is not proof that any of them is right; S.T.A.R. and SciPy share the force formula.

No quantum advantage is assumed anywhere on this site. Where a quantum method is studied, it is compared with the best classical method, and the classical one is the default.
