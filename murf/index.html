MURF — Multi-fidelity Unified Reality Fabric Research

Digital twins combining deterministic reference solvers, fast surrogate models and independent validation bounds, with explicit uncertainty intervals. Under active research: this page states what exists today and what does not.

Why a multi-fidelity digital twin

Full physics simulation is accurate and slow; a fast surrogate is swift and valid only within its verified domain. A multi-fidelity twin coordinates both: it evaluates rapidly where the surrogate is demonstrably valid, invokes reference solvers where it is not, and states explicitly which layer produced each output.

How a MURF result is labelled

  • SIMULATED output of a model
  • PREDICTED output of a surrogate, with its uncertainty
  • CROSSCHECKED agrees with an independent implementation within a stated tolerance
  • FORMALLY VERIFIED a property proved by a formal tool, and only that property
  • EXPERIMENTALLY VALIDATED compared with real measurements

These are not synonyms, and a result carries only the labels its evidence supports.

Twin 0: collision probability between two satellites

The first twin starts from an engine already measured, which is not yet a public package. S.T.A.R.'s two-dimensional collision probability agrees within 1e-7 (relative) with the adjusted value of NASA CARA on 53 of 53 real conjunctions and with Orekit on 48 of 48. Against a different CARA value, computed with another encounter definition, an earlier pre-registered rule of 1e-6 was not met on 11 of 48 cases; the Research page reports it. On 29 of those 53 cases the assumptions of the two-dimensional method do not hold. The question of Twin 0 is whether a second, more faithful level can be called only where the first one stops being valid, with a rule measured in advance.

The twin's sheet, written before the benchmark

Systemtwo objects in Earth orbit at their closest approach
Inputs and unitspositions and velocities (km, km/s), position covariances (km²), hard-body radius (m), from a conjunction data message
Referencethe Monte Carlo published by NASA CARA for each case (two-body motion), with its number of trials and its interval
Data53 real conjunctions with the probability published by NASA CARA
Levelslevel 0: the two-dimensional engine star.ssa.pc2d, cross-checked, not yet a public package; level 1: our own Monte Carlo on two-body orbits, written from the published description of the method
Validity of the fast levelencounter short compared with the orbits, motion straight during it, covariances constant: measured indicators, not assumed
Uncertaintya 95 % interval for every Monte Carlo value; level 1 is accepted only if its interval overlaps NASA's on at least 90 % of the cases
When the fast level is not trusteda rule chosen on one half of the cases (split fixed by a hash before any run), frozen with its fingerprint, then applied once to the other half
Independent checksNASA CARA values, Orekit
Metricsdecisions at the thresholds 1e-7, 1e-5 and 1e-4 that differ from NASA's; cases sent to level 1; time per case; error on the logarithm of the probability
Result and limitspublished with the cases where the method fails
When it does not knowit says so and returns the reference value or no value

Where it stands

What MURF does not claim

It does not replace CPUs, GPUs, CUDA, PyTorch or cloud computing: it uses them where they are strong. No speed-up or accuracy figure is published before a benchmark on declared hardware with its baseline. A fast answer is not proof of accuracy, and a model's own uncertainty is not a guaranteed error bound.
