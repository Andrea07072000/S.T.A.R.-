Failure observatory

What went wrong, how it was found and what changed. Publishing failures is part of the method: a result is worth trusting only if its failures are visible too.

Corrected after a review (2026-10-19)

Five separate reviews of this site, each from a different point of view — a private user, a small business, an engineer, a designer and an auditor of claims — found these problems. All were corrected before publication.

  • The home page said every result is checked against independent implementations. Not every package was compared with two; the claim was changed to what the evidence shows.
  • The orbit-propagation showcase said three programs by different teams give one answer. Two share the same force formula, and S.T.A.R.'s integrator is the least precise of the three (about 1.5 cm, while the references agree within the 0.1 mm recording precision). Now stated.
  • The processor lab called its times upper bounds and modelled a CPU without vector units. Best-case times are lower bounds on time; the model now includes vector units and is checked against a real measurement on our laptop.
  • In the Workspace, a number typed with spaces ("1 2 3 4") was silently read as 1234, and the value 0 was treated as empty. Both were found by our own tests before release and fixed.
  • The ground-track shift per lap used the wrong period (anomalistic instead of nodal). Found by a test that measures the shift numerically; fixed.

Claims that failed their own test

Each was written down with a pass/fail rule before the experiment ran. These are the ones that failed.

Collision probability vs NASA CARA

Rule: every case within 1e-6 relative. Result: 11 of 48 cases above, largest 2.9e-3. Not explained away: published as failed.

Measured — failed (pre-registered rule)

A quantum-safe key exchange in one telecommand frame

Rule: fits one CCSDS TC frame. Result: 1,088-byte ciphertext against at most 1,016 data bytes — at least two frames.

Computed — negative answer

A geometry-only flag for misuse of the 2D collision method

Rule: recall of NASA's own flags above a set threshold. Result: recall 0.34 (10 of 29) — precise, but misses most cases.

Measured — failed (pre-registered rule)
