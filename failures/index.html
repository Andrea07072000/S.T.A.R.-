Failure observatory

What went wrong, how it was detected and what changed as a result. Publishing failures is part of the method: a result deserves trust only if its failures are visible as well.

Corrected after a review (2026-10-19)

Five separate reviews of this site, each from a different perspective (a private user, a small business, an engineer, a designer and an auditor of claims), identified these problems. All were corrected before publication.

  • The home page stated that every result is checked against independent implementations. Not every package had been compared with two; the statement was reduced to what the evidence supports.
  • The orbit-propagation showcase stated that three programs by different teams give one answer. Two share the same force formula, and S.T.A.R.'s integrator is the least precise of the three (about 1.5 cm, while the references agree within the 0.1 mm recording precision). This is now stated.
  • The processor lab described its times as upper bounds and modelled a CPU without vector units. Best-case times are lower bounds on execution time; the model now includes vector units and is compared with a real measurement on our laptop.
  • In the Workspace, a number typed with spaces ("1 2 3 4") was silently read as 1234, and the value 0 was treated as empty. Both were detected by our own tests before release and fixed.
  • The ground-track shift per orbit used the wrong period (anomalistic instead of nodal). Detected by a test that measures the shift numerically; fixed.

Claims that did not pass their own test

Each claim was recorded with a pass/fail rule before the experiment was run. These are the claims that did not pass.

Collision probability vs NASA CARA

Rule: every case within 1e-6 relative. Result: 11 of 48 cases above, largest 2.9e-3. No post-hoc justification: published as not met.

Measured — pre-registered rule not met

A quantum-safe key exchange in one telecommand frame

Rule: fits within one CCSDS TC frame. Result: a 1,088-byte ciphertext against at most 1,016 data bytes; at least two frames.

Computed — negative answer

A geometry-only flag for misuse of the 2D collision method

Rule: recall of NASA's own flags above a fixed threshold. Result: recall 0.34 (10 of 29): precise, but misses most cases.
